# Commit and pull request English

Every commit message, pull request title, and pull request description in this repository must read as natural, fluent native English. Do not rewrite commits that have already been pushed.

## Subject

Write one imperative sentence. Capitalise it. Aim for about 50 characters, and never more than 72. Do not put a full stop at the end. Keep normal grammar and articles (`the`, `a`, `an`). Do not stack nouns or list keywords after a colon. Describe the effect on the product, not the process used to get there.

## Body

Leave one blank line after the subject. Wrap the body at 72 characters. Write two to five plain sentences that say what was wrong, what changed, and why. Every sentence must have a subject and a verb. Do not start a line with a clipped fragment such as `Notes` or `Keeps`.

## Words to avoid

Do not use private shorthand. Do not mention sample or fixture codes unless they are essential, and then say what the diagram is. Do not mention review rounds. Do not use nicknames such as `afters`, `one-inside edges`, `quality widen`, or `pierce`. Prefer plain words such as `comparison images` and `crosses`.

Never mention people or agents. State the requirement itself.

## Where numbers belong

Test counts and review results belong in the pull request conversation, not in commit bodies.

## Examples

`Tighten shared route quality: clearance, colinear collapse, labels.` becomes `Keep edges clear of unrelated boxes and labels on their own line`.

`Re-render layout-fix afters after the sample 03 quality widen.` becomes `Regenerate the comparison images after the routing changes`.

`Stop the after-docs job failing when only the PNG pixels differ.` becomes `Ignore PNG pixel differences in the comparison image check`.
